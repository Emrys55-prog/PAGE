Water for Tharaka, paid by the litre.

Say how much water you need and where. A local provider accepts at their own price per litre, delivers it, and you pay them directly.

How it works

  1. Sign up. Choose Client if you need water, or Water provider if you supply it. You choose once. The type cannot be changed later.
  2. Clients post a request. Give the place, the litres, and the most you will pay per litre if you want a limit.
  3. A provider accepts. The provider charges their own price per litre. Litres times price is your total.
  4. Water arrives, you pay. Pay the provider in cash or by M-Pesa. They mark the job paid.

Why this is needed

Around Tharaka-Nithi, water is scarce, sometimes unsafe, and expensive to get. Finding a supplier and agreeing a price should not be one more problem.

Water scarcity
Long dry spells leave many homes and farms without enough water. Women often walk far to fetch it, which takes time away from work and school.
Unsafe water
With piped water costly or missing, many families use boreholes. Studies in Tharaka-Nithi found bacteria in some of them, which can make people sick.
Fights over water
Rivers are drying up while more people need water for homes and farms. This can cause conflict between neighbours.
Costly irrigation
Farmers say the price of irrigation water is too high, which hurts the crops that families depend on.
Prices that swing
Where piped water runs short, vendors in Kenyan towns often charge KSh 20 to 50 for a 20-litre jerrycan, several times what regulated kiosks charge. A visible price per litre helps people compare.

Ideas that can help

  • Rainwater harvesting: catch rain from roofs and store it in tanks.
  • Treat your water: boil it, or use a filter or chlorine, before drinking.
  • Store water well: keep it in clean, covered containers.
  • Save water: fix leaks and do not waste water on campus.
  • Protect rivers: plant trees and keep waste out of streams.
  • Smart water kiosks: the county has started installing kiosks where people load a token with M-Pesa and pay for the litres they take.

What students can do

Small actions add up. Turn off taps, report leaks, share what you learn about safe water with your family, and join clean-up or tree-planting days.
